# Client proposal and copy contract

## Proposal structure

1. **Executive answer:** what should be done, for whom, and why now.
2. **Business context:** objective, stage, constraints, and success definition.
3. **Evidence and diagnosis:** facts, observed gaps, assumptions, and implications.
4. **Audience system:** ICP, buyer roles, triggers, journey, objections, and disqualifiers.
5. **Strategic choice:** positioning, offer, message hierarchy, channel portfolio, and exclusions.
6. **Execution:** workstreams, assets, milestones, owner/RACI, dependencies, and cadence.
7. **Commercials:** scope, quantity, period, unit logic, taxes, terms, and out-of-scope work.
8. **Measurement:** baseline, KPI definition, data source, target logic, thresholds, and cadence.
9. **Risk controls:** compliance, approvals, platform dependency, evidence gaps, and contingency.
10. **Next step:** the single decision or action needed from the client.

Keep strategy, deliverables, quotation, and KPI mathematically consistent.

## Communication brief

Define audience and awareness; moment, channel, and reading context; desired belief and next behavior; one main promise and available proof; objections and prohibited claims; voice, length, format, and CTA.

## Copy workflow

1. Reuse real problem language from interviews, search behavior, sales calls, or supplied data.
2. Choose one angle: pain relief, desired outcome, mechanism, comparison, proof, grounded urgency, or identity.
3. Draft: hook → relevance → promise → mechanism → proof → objection answer → CTA.
4. Adapt to the format: clear outcome and proof for landing pages; one narrative/action for email; native relevance for social; personalized and low-friction outbound; evidence-led newsworthiness for PR.
5. Produce variants that test meaningful hypotheses, not synonym swaps.
6. Record factual and performance claims with evidence, qualification, and approval owner.

Remove or rewrite unsupported claims. Avoid guarantees, false scarcity, fabricated testimonials, unsupported superlatives, and undisclosed commercial relationships.

## Acceptance questions

- Who exactly are we trying to move?
- What should they believe and do?
- Why should they believe us?
- Where and when will the message reach them?
- What will be produced, by whom, and by when?
- How will we know whether to stop, revise, or scale?
